# Which Warm-up?

Well, the question is better stated as "how long the warm-up time should be in the simulation", and unfortunately the answer is _not_ straightforward  lol

## Why?

The main objective of the simulation is to study information diffusion of a post with the use of the cascades. In order to accurately do this, we must discard all the posts created on the _warm-up_ phase, as the simulation is not performing as it should.

In the warm-up phase posts are just generated by all users. No reposts nor checking. Evaluating the lifetime of those posts would be incorrect, as the simulation behaviour would be missrepresented. The warm-up phase is needed for the users not to get inmediately into boredom: not any posts to see, therefore the user disconnects. Therefore, the dichotomy is: if the warmu-up is to short, the boredom will truncate all cascade information, and if the warm-up is too long, all generated cascades must be ignored.


## How?

We can measure the impact of the boredom time with the following three metrics:
- histogram of boredom ended sessions. This number at the beginning should be very small.
- % of actions over warm-up posts during the simulation: this must start very high, as there is no new posts being created, but decrease very fast in order to give room to the other posts.
- new post interactions over time: we should see a growing trend, as time goes by there should be more and more as time passes, which implies warm-up posts are decreasing.

From this metrics the graphs are the best way to see them. The `boredom_timeline.png` shows the amount of bored users per time, the `warmup-attention-decay.png` shows the amount of non warmup posts that get shown and the `new_post_traction.png` shows how new posts are gaining traction.



## Results

All experiments run with 40 repetitions, 4 workers, on the 10K monotonous network with the following datasets.

| Warmup | Boredom% | Avg sesh | Post-warmup | % Warmup attn | % Prolif | Indirect% |
|--------|----------|----------|-------------|---------------|----------|-----------|
| 0      | 85.9%    | 45.0     | 16,335      | 0.0%          | 40.3%    | 54.4%     |
| 25     | 47.4%    | 180.3    | 162,927     | 91.3%         | 6.4%     | 3.0%      |
| 50     | 46.8%    | 182.0    | 162,426     | 90.3%         | 6.0%     | 2.5%      |
| 100    | 45.7%    | 184.7    | 171,217     | 92.1%         | 5.4%     | 2.5%      |
| 250    | 43.6%    | 192.4    | 176,498     | 94.2%         | 4.2%     | 1.4%      |
| 500    | 40.6%    | 199.0    | 184,106     | 96.0%         | 2.9%     | 0.8%      |
| 750    | 37.8%    | 209.5    | 185,689     | 97.0%         | 2.3%     | 0.5%      |
| 1000   | 36.5%    | 213.6    | 183,604     | 97.4%         | 1.9%     | 0.4%      |

**warmup=100** is the recommended value: boredom drops to a manageable 45.7% (from 85.9% at zero), while retaining over 170K analyzable posts. Warmup=0 has the best cascade metrics but 86% of sessions end from boredom — users barely engage. Warmup ≥ 250 brings marginal boredom improvement while steadily degrading cascade proliferation.

First important finding from the table is that the _warm-up_ concept works: zero warm-up makes 85% of boredom sessions, and the amount of posts generated is very few. In contrast, with just 25 ticks of warm-up, the boedom drops by half (47.4%) and the number of posts is 10 times bigger (162,927), making a far more healthy simulation.


The most important finding and the answer to the main question is that from 25 ticks of boredom to 1000, the boredom % just decreases 10%, but the amount of non repost post changes drastically: with 1000 ticks of warmup, almost all the posts at the end of the simulation are still warmup posts propagating, and very few of the normal ones.

Also, this proves that regardless of the statuionary behaviour of the simulation, it makes sense to run the simulation for a longer time in order to obtain more and bigger cascades :)
