# Which Warm-up?

Well, the question is better stated as "how long the warm-up time should be in the simulation", and unfortunately the answer is _not_ straightforward  lol

## Why?

The main objective of the simulation is to study information diffusion of a post with the use of the cascades. In order to accurately do this, we must discard all the posts created on the _warm-up_ phase, as the simulation is not performing as it should.

In the warm-up phase posts are just generated by all users. No reposts nor checking. Evaluating the lifetime of those posts would be incorrect, as the simulation behaviour would be missrepresented. The warm-up phase is needed for the users not to get inmediately into boredom: not any posts to see, therefore the user disconnects. Therefore, the dichotomy is: if the warm-up is too short, the boredom will truncate all cascade information, and if the warm-up is too long, all generated cascades must be ignored.

> **Implementation note:** warmup post-creation events are scheduled at `uniform(0, warmup_time) + creation_delay` (where `creation_delay` = 1.0 tick). This means any `warmup_time < creation_delay` produces *zero* warmup posts — the first event lands past the warmup horizon and the stage‑one loop exits immediately. Warmup values of 0, 0.5, and 1.0 are therefore all effectively identical to warmup=0.


## How?

We can measure the impact of the boredom time with the following three metrics:
- histogram of boredom ended sessions. This number at the beginning should be very small.
- % of actions over warm-up posts during the simulation: this must start very high, as there is no new posts being created, but decrease very fast in order to give room to the other posts.
- new post interactions over time: we should see a growing trend, as time goes by there should be more and more as time passes, which implies warm-up posts are decreasing.

From this metrics the graphs are the best way to see them. The `boredom_timeline.png` shows the amount of bored users per time, the `warmup-attention-decay.png` shows the amount of non warmup posts that get shown and the `new_post_traction.png` shows how new posts are gaining traction.

### Key findings

**Boredom is stubbornly high regardless of warmup.** Warmup=0 is definitionally excluded (no warmup phase at all, model doesn't behave as intended), and across the viable range (2–100), boredom only drops from 49.3% to 45.7% — a mere 3.6 pp. The warmup helps keep users engaged, but it won't fix boredom on its own.

**The real signal is cascade structure on simulation posts.** What changes dramatically with warmup is whether the actual simulation-generated posts get to form cascades. At warmup=2, 7.1% of reposts are indirect and 8.9% of cascades are multi-level — healthy viral propagation. But between warmup=2 and warmup=3, indirect% collapses to 1.6% (a 78% drop). By warmup=5 it's 0.7%, and by warmup=100 it's 0.2%. Warmup posts flood the timelines and steal the interactions that should go to the posts we actually want to study.

**The trade-off:** higher warmup gives marginally less boredom but kills cascade depth. Lower warmup keeps cascades intact at the cost of slightly more boredom. Since boredom barely improves anyway, the right call is to preserve cascade structure.

### Recommendation

**warmup=2.** It's the smallest viable warmup (any value < 2 is killed by `creation_delay`) and strikes the best balance: boredom is manageable (49.3%), sessions are healthy (174 ticks), and simulation posts still form meaningful cascades (7.1% indirect, 8.9% multilevel). Going higher sacrifices cascade depth for negligible boredom improvement.
